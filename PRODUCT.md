# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters evaluating internship candidates and professors or research mentors considering undergraduate collaborators. They arrive with limited time, high pattern-recognition for "serious" vs. "generic", and are looking for signal about technical depth, follow-through, and trajectory. Recruiters scan for stack breadth and shipped work; professors scan for research curiosity and intellectual honesty.

## Product Purpose

Chase Phung's personal portfolio. The site exists to establish credibility with high-signal visitors — not to impress everyone, but to convince the right people quickly. Success looks like a recruiter reaching out or a professor agreeing to a meeting after a single visit.

## Brand Personality

Ambitious, focused, capable. The voice is direct and understated — no hedging, no self-promotion through adjectives. Confidence comes from the work itself, not from how it's framed. The site feels like it was made by someone who knows what they're doing and doesn't need to announce it.

## Anti-references

- Generic developer portfolios: centered hero, pastel gradients, floating cards with icons, "Hi, I'm a developer who loves coffee"
- SaaS AI slop: glassmorphism, gradient text, hero-metric templates, identical card grids, neon accents
- Resume-dump sites: just a styled CV with no editorial point of view
- "Creative developer" aesthetic: brutalist-for-shock, neon-on-black, excessive motion

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't credential** — let shipped work and specific details carry authority. No superlatives, no vague claims.
2. **Earned sparseness** — every element on the page has a reason. Whitespace is intentional, not accidental.
3. **Signal depth over breadth** — one well-described project beats five bullet points. Quality of thinking over volume.
4. **Expert confidence** — the design makes no apologies. Choices are committed, not hedged.
5. **The person is part of the work** — jazz, national parks, hackathons for the food. Humanity isn't separate from the technical story; it makes the technical story more credible.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. No known specific user needs beyond standard contrast and keyboard navigation.
